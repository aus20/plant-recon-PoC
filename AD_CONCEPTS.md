# Three advertising concepts

Part two of the case study. Three films, three different emotional registers - not one
idea shot three ways. Each rests on something the product actually does, verified against
the working app rather than invented for the script.

**The product truth they draw on**

| | Measured |
|---|---|
| Photo to answer | 5 – 6 seconds end to end |
| What comes back | Name, family, care, and a graded toxicity verdict |
| Toxicity | `none` / `mild` / `serious`, per pets and per people |
| Honesty | A calibrated confidence and the runner-up species, not a confident guess |
| Memory | Every identification kept on the device |

The third concept is the strongest, and the reason is in that table: the toxicity field is
the one competitors mostly leave out, and it is the only one that answers a question people
ask with their hands already reaching for the plant.

---

## 1. "What is that?"
**Register: curiosity.** 15 seconds. TikTok / Reels

**Hook (0–3s).** A hand pushes a branch aside. Extreme close-up of a leaf nobody can name.
No logo, no music sting, no app. Just the question everyone has had on a walk.

| Time | Shot | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Macro on an unfamiliar leaf, shallow focus, wind moving it | Ambient park sound. On screen: **what is that** |
| 3–6s | Phone rises into frame, viewfinder frames the leaf, shutter | *VO:* "You've walked past this a hundred times." |
| 6–9s | Result card resolves: name, family, care | *VO:* "Six seconds." On screen: **Wood forget-me-not · Myosotis sylvatica** |
| 9–13s | Walking on; two more quick identifications, jump-cut | *VO:* "Now you know the whole street." |
| 13–15s | App mark on dark ground | On screen: **Plant Identifier. Point. Know.** |

**CTA.** *Point. Know.* App Store badge.

**Why it works.** It sells the feeling of the world becoming legible. No problem, no pain -
just the small pleasure of the unnamed becoming named. It is the concept that survives being
watched with the sound off, which is how most of them will be watched.

## 2. "You didn't kill it. You misread it."
**Register: guilt, then relief.** 20 seconds. Instagram / YouTube

**Hook (0–3s).** A dead plant in a bin bag, shot like a small funeral. The line lands before
anything else does: *"This wasn't your fault."*

| Time | Shot | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | A browned, collapsed plant, top-down, kitchen light | Single piano note. On screen: **this wasn't your fault** |
| 3–7s | Cuts of care that went wrong: full sun on a shade plant, a heavy pour into a saucer | *VO:* "You watered it. You moved it into the light." |
| 7–10s | The same plant, alive, weeks earlier. Phone photographs it | *VO:* "It just wasn't that kind of plant." |
| 10–15s | Result card: **Light — Partial shade. Water — Keep evenly moist.** Labels land one by one | *VO:* "Four words each. That's the whole difference." |
| 15–18s | The plant thriving on a shaded sill, same room, later | Piano resolves |
| 18–20s | App mark | On screen: **Know what it needs. Before it tells you.** |

**CTA.** *Know what it needs.* App Store badge.

**Why it works.** Nearly everyone in the audience has killed a houseplant, and everyone
privately suspects it was their own carelessness. The film moves the blame to a missing fact
and then hands over that fact. It also demonstrates the redesigned care labels, which are
genuinely four words long - the film would not have been shootable before that change.

## 3. "Can the cat eat it?"
**Register: fear, then protection.** 20 seconds. TikTok / Reels

**Hook (0–3s).** A cat's face, very close, moving toward a leaf. It opens its mouth. Cut to
black at the exact moment before the bite.

| Time | Shot | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Cat approaching a houseplant, slow, deliberate. Mouth opens. Hard cut to black | Room tone. On screen: **wait** |
| 3–6s | Hand scoops the cat away; the other hand is already photographing the plant | *VO:* "You have about four seconds to know." |
| 6–11s | Result card, toxicity banner first: amber **Mild irritant for pets** | *VO:* "This one, an upset stomach." |
| 11–15s | Second plant, second scan. Banner turns red: **Toxic — keep away from pets** | *VO:* "This one, the vet." |
| 15–18s | The red-flagged plant moved to a high shelf, out of reach. Cat settles elsewhere | *VO:* "Same shelf. Very different night." |
| 18–20s | App mark | On screen: **Plant Identifier. Know before they do.** |

**CTA.** *Know before they do.* App Store badge.

**Why it works.** It is the only one of the three with a stake, and the stake is somebody
you love rather than a plant you bought. It is also the only one a competitor cannot copy
cheaply: it needs a graded toxicity verdict, and the amber-to-red beat in the middle is the
whole argument for grading it. A binary "toxic / not toxic" makes that film impossible -
either every plant is a red alarm and the viewer stops believing it, or the mild ones read
as safe and the promise is a lie.
